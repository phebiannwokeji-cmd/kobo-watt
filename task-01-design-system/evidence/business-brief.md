# Kobo Watt: Business Brief

## The business
Kobo Watt is a fictional rent-to-own solar and inverter service in Ikeja, Lagos. A customer pays a deposit, then any amount from a one-day minimum toward the rest. Each confirmed payment reduces what she owes and buys days of paid access at her daily rate. When paid days run out, one grace period follows, then access expires. Full payment ends expiry. Access means what our records say; it does not guarantee electricity or device health. Numbers and rules are in business-decisions.md.

Out of scope: real money, device firmware, telemetry and physical locking.

## The person who uses it
Adaobi, 38, runs a hair salon and is paying off an inverter. Provisional persona, not interviewed.
- **Age range:** 25 to 55.
- **Device:** mid-range Android phone, cracked corner, used in sunlight.
- **Connection:** patchy mobile data that drops, often when the power is off and she needs the app most.
- **Technical level:** comfortable with WhatsApp and bank transfers. Not with settings, jargon or long forms.

## The one thing that must work
When her power is off, Adaobi sees within seconds what our records say about her access and exactly how much to pay, then pays and watches it confirm. Offline, an already-open screen shows a read-only last-known status with an "as of" time, cleared on logout or after a set time. Paying needs signal.

## Three questions only this business can answer
1. "Why is my power off, and since when?" Only our records can say.
2. "How much do I pay to switch on, or to stay on until Friday?" Only her plan, credit and grace produce that figure.
3. "Why was I charged ₦10,000 on 3 October?" Only her own payment records show what it bought.

## Why the interface must feel this way
**Steady, plain and fair**, like a printed receipt or a prepaid meter, never a flashy fintech app. Adaobi opens it at her worst moment: a client mid-appointment, and a worry she was locked after paying. Calm, exact numbers repair that trust.

**This drives the typography:**
- One sturdy open sans-serif, generous x-height, legible small in glare.
- Large tabular numerals, so amounts and days line up.
- A true ₦ glyph, checked on real Android.
- Two weights, regular and bold. No display faces, italics or all-caps.
- Light font payload with system fallbacks.
