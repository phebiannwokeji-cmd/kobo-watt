# Kobo Watt: Reconciled Demonstration Pricing Model

I use an internally reconciled assessment scenario, not a verified commercial offer. I distinguish my supplied assumptions from calculations. The numerical scenario was revised on 25 October 2026.

## 1. Contract rules selected for the prototype

| Item | Naira | Kobo |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sale price | 3,641,200 | 364,120,000 |
| Exact required deposit | 1,092,400 | 109,240,000 |
| Post-deposit balance | 2,548,800 | 254,880,000 |
| Daily access rate | 3,540 | 354,000 |
| Minimum access payment (k = 1 × R) | 3,540 | 354,000 |

The deposit is approximately 30%, not exactly 30%: exact 30% is ₦1,092,360. The additional ₦40 leaves a balance exactly divisible into 720 daily-rate payments. Require one exact deposit payment; no partial deposits in this prototype. It reduces the balance but grants no time. The next, separate access payment starts access. A smaller final balance can be paid exactly. A paid-off account stops expiring even when previously purchased time remains.

₦3,641,200 − ₦1,092,400 = ₦2,548,800.
₦2,548,800 / ₦3,540 = 720 paid days.

Do not label this 24 calendar months. These are 720 daily-rate equivalents, with flexible prepayment and unpaid gaps. Gaps do not increase customer debt, but can increase the business's costs. No fixed completion deadline, no promise that finance costs stay constant over arbitrary elapsed durations, and no live profitability claim are made.

Non-acquisition allowances total ₦795,458. The auditable formula is:

Residual contribution = sale price − acquisition cost − financing allowance − warranty allowance − operating allowance − default allocation.

At the acquisition hypothesis, the remainder is ₦362,645, or 9.9595% of the sale price. Call it modelled pre-tax residual contribution under the listed assumptions, not validated profit. The target is approximately 10%, not an achieved exact 10% margin.

## 4. Default loading: conditional arithmetic only

The supplied ₦397,458 loss per default has no supporting recovery calculation. Retain it only as a labelled hypothetical net loss, not an observed loss.

A possible reconstruction is allocation per completing contract = hypothetical loss × default share / completing share:

₦397,458 × 0.15 / 0.85 = ₦70,139.65, rounded to ₦70,140.

This reconstruction assumes that ₦397,458 is the complete net loss for a defaulted contract and that default costs are not counted elsewhere. It does not establish those assumptions. ₦70,140 is not simply 15% of the price or acquisition cost.

The previous claim “survives defaults up to 52%” is withdrawn. A simplified two-outcome model can yield 52.13%, but it is not evidence of cash-flow survival. No default tolerance is approved for business decisions.

To validate losses, build dated cash flows for each scenario: acquisition and installation outflows; deposit and instalments received; funding charges and operating expenses incurred; recovery/collection costs; and net resale or insurance proceeds, with no double counting. Specify when default occurs, how likely recovery is, how long it takes and what equipment condition affects proceeds. Compare recoveries and outflows consistently in time.

A 25% deposit would be ₦910,300, or ₦182,100 less than the selected deposit. With every other cash flow unchanged, that increases loss by ₦182,100. It does not prove the absolute loss or account for changed customer behaviour. The deposit choice needs affordability and recovery research.

## 5. Financing model required before commercial approval

₦542,818 is now explicitly a budget allowance, not a derived interest expense. Do not infer a lender rate from it.

Obtain the funding rate, whether fixed/variable, calculation frequency, fees, principal financed, drawdown dates, repayment obligations and how customer receipts reduce borrowing. Then calculate borrowing balance and charges over time, including delayed and missed customer payments. Add all applicable funding fees. Compare total modelled charges with the allowance and test longer elapsed terms.

Until those inputs exist, use the fixed-budget scenario for assessment demonstrations only. Do not invent lender terms to make the original total match.

## 6. Affordability: calculations, not a conclusion

My hypotheses: gross revenue ₦60,000 and business net income ₦15,000 per working day; 26 working days per illustrative 30-day period.

- Thirty days of access costs ₦106,200.
- Spread over 26 working days: ₦4,084.62 per working day.
- That is 27.23% of assumed net and 6.81% of assumed gross.
- Deposit equals 72.83 working days of all assumed net, or 18.21 working days of gross revenue.
- At 26 working days per month, the deposit equals 2.80 months of all assumed net. This is not a savings-time forecast: household needs and other debts must be paid too.

Define net income before using it: record business costs, current energy costs, existing debt, household withdrawals and whether any claimed energy savings are already included. Available cash for this plan is what remains after required spending and obligations. Do not add energy savings without evidence or count them twice.

Validate with actual customer records and consent, including weak trading periods, not one good day. A supplier/operator interview alone does not verify customer income. No affordability approval is made from these ratios.

## 7. Scope of my pricing evidence

I use this model for assessment demonstrations only. I have not validated supplier quotes, funding terms, customer cash flows or full live-service costs. I do not present these calculations as a commercial offer.

## 8. Entry 2 fee sensitivity — checked 25 October 2026

Entry 2 supersedes the former fixed ₦1,000 floor. Minimum = k × contract R; k = 1. The minimum buys one day, but amounts above the floor need not be whole-day multiples. The final-balance and exact-deposit exceptions remain.

Paystack's published standard Nigerian local transaction pricing is 1.5% + ₦100, with the flat ₦100 waived below ₦2,500 and a ₦2,000 transaction cap. Checked against https://paystack.com/pricing and https://support.paystack.com/en/articles/2130306 on 25 October 2026. This is an illustrative channel/rate, not provider selection or a universal rate across Paystack products. Recheck selected channel, contract terms and any additional applicable charges before launch.

Assume the business absorbs these charges; the customer receives full gross-payment credit. Never deduct provider fees from access credit while claiming the minimum buys a complete day.

- One ₦3,540 payment: ₦153.10 fee; effective rate 4.3249%.
- One ₦1,770 payment: ₦26.55 fee; effective rate 1.5%.
- Two half-day payments would cost ₦53.10 in total, versus ₦153.10 for one full-day payment. This comparison illustrates the price of the chosen convenience; half-day payments are not allowed by Entry 2.
- 720 separate daily payments: 720 × ₦153.10 = ₦110,232.
- One deposit paid through the same channel: capped fee ₦2,000.
- Combined illustrative provider cost: ₦112,232. This is a specified payment-pattern scenario, not a universal maximum or a measured contract cost. Failed attempts, reversals, additional charges and alternative channels require separate treatment.

The earlier fixed-budget model excluded provider costs. If these fees are additional, the ₦362,645 residual falls to ₦250,413 (approximately 6.8772% of price). At the unverified ₦2,753,012 acquisition amount, the ₦92,730 residual becomes a ₦19,502 loss. Holding every other allowance and this payment pattern fixed, conditional acquisition break-even becomes ₦2,733,510.

These are scenario calculations, not validated profit or a recommendation to change the agreed price. Confirm whether fees are already embedded in any allowance before adding them, to avoid double-counting. The approximately 10% target is not met in this additional-fee scenario. Entries 3A and 4B are now approved prototype policies: one fixed 48-hour grace window after the first paid-access expiry, and a minimal memory-only snapshot lasting at most five minutes after the last authenticated account refresh. Neither has yet been validated through operator/customer research or implementation tests.


## 9. Selected grace policy and cost boundary

The one-time 48-hour grace window never resets or extends after payment. It adds no customer debt and does not reduce the purchase balance. It represents up to two days of unpaid access entitlement, equivalent to ₦7,080 at R = ₦3,540, not an established cash expense. Do not automatically subtract that rate-equivalent value as a cost or count it as revenue. Funding/service costs and completion timing require separate modelling. The approved offline display policy creates no access credit and cannot confirm payments.
